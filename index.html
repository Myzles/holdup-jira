Holdup: Time in Status for Jira

Holdup shows how long Jira issues spend in each status, how long work takes from start to finish, and which issues have stopped moving. Install it and open a project. There is nothing to set up.

Where to find it

  • Project report: in any project, open the Time in Status tab (it may sit under More).
  • Issue sidebar: open an issue and expand Time in Status in the right-hand panel to see that issue's history.

What the report shows

  • Cycle time: from the first time an issue enters an in-progress status to the last time it reaches done. The card shows the median and the time 85% of issues finish within.
  • Lead time: from creation to done. When Jira has a resolution date, that date is used.
  • Average time in each status: to-do statuses in grey, in-progress statuses in blue. The slowest in-progress status is named above the chart. Time after an issue is done is left out.
  • Stuck now: open issues sitting in the same in-progress status for longer than your threshold (5 days unless you change it).
  • Issues table: every issue with its current status, time in that status, cycle time, lead time and a column per status. Click a column to sort, click a key to open the issue.

Filters

  • Issues: open or recently updated, finished in the period, or open only.
  • Period: 14 days to 2 years. Type: one issue type or all.
  • Extra JQL: anything Jira search accepts, for example assignee = currentUser() or labels = backend.
  • Skip weekends: leaves Saturdays and Sundays out of every duration, using your computer's time zone.
  • Show: mixed units (3d 4h), days or hours.

One report covers up to the 5,000 newest matching issues. If a project has more, the report says so and you can narrow the filters.

Export

Export CSV downloads every row in the report, with durations as plain numbers in days (or hours if you picked hours), ready for Excel or Google Sheets.

Stuck-work emails

Project admins can open the Alerts tab, set the stuck threshold and switch on emails. Once a day Holdup looks for assigned issues in an in-progress status that haven't changed status for that many days, and asks Jira to email the assignee. Each issue is emailed at most once a week. Emails go through Jira's own notifications, so people only receive what Jira would normally send them. Emails count calendar days and skip unassigned issues. The issue sidebar shows elapsed time and ignores the weekend setting in the report.

Permissions

The report and sidebar read issues as the person looking at them, so nobody sees an issue they couldn't already open. Holdup cannot edit issues.

Scopes requested: read:jira-work (issues and history), read:jira-user (assignee names for display), storage:app (alert settings and the last email time per issue), send:notification:jira (the stuck-work email, sent by Jira). Nothing is sent to us or any outside service.

For example, a 50-user site pays 50 x $0.30 = $15 a month, or $150 a year. Atlassian's pricing tab shows the exact figure for your site.
